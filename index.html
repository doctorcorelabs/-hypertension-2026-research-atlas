01 / THE RESEARCH QUESTIONFROM TREATMENT TO TRADE-OFFS

There is no single
safety story.

Treating resistant hypertension means looking beyond a number on a blood pressure monitor.

This project compares the reported adverse-event patterns of newer non-steroidal MRA, ARNI, and dual ERA therapies against conventional steroidal MRAs. The focus is not “which drug wins,” but how electrolyte, endocrine, hemodynamic, and fluid-related signals diverge.

Clinical context: resistant hypertension is generally uncontrolled blood pressure despite three appropriate agents, including a diuretic, or control requiring four or more agents. This FAERS analysis does not establish that every included report meets that definition.

01No exposure denominator +

FAERS does not contain the total number of patients who took each drug. Report percentages are not adverse-event incidence, and ROR is not relative clinical risk.

02Association is not causation +

Reporting disproportionality can flag a pattern, but cannot prove a drug caused an event. Mechanistic explanations are plausibility arguments, not causal findings.

03Different patients, different indications +

Chronic kidney disease, heart failure, concomitant therapies, and prescribing selection can influence both drug exposure and reported events. The analysis does not confirm a strictly resistant-hypertension cohort.

04Missingness and reporting bias +

Underreporting, stimulated reporting, duplicate submissions, and incomplete dates affect interpretation. For example, only 57 of 291 finerenone hyperkalemia reports contribute to that onset analysis.

05No treatment recommendation +

This site explains a research project. It does not recommend starting, replacing, or stopping therapy. Patient-specific decisions require clinical assessment and appropriate guidelines.

Source consistency & provenance notes +

Displayed values are reported estimates. This atlas transcribes the supplied abstract and manuscript; it does not independently authenticate the raw FDA records or certify the analytical pipeline.

    The abstract is a supplied conference-submission document, not verification of publication or peer review. Its causal language has been softened here to reflect the limitations of spontaneous-reporting evidence.
